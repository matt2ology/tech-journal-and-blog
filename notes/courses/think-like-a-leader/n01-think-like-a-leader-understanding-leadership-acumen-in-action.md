## TL;DR (too long; didn't read)

> [!tldr]
>
> - **Leadership is not 1 fixed style - it is dynamic, contextual, and situational**
> - ==Acumen== is both vibes and direction. It isn't concerned about knowing what the right answer is, but to build the pattern recognition and experience to orient team and situation to a better direction and outcome.
> - Good leaders know when to use different leadership styles when the moment best fits (i.e. **what leadership style does the situation need?**)
>   - **Coaching (the process):** helps people think - builds better judgment and capability over time
>     - Is slow - asking questions in the moment
>     - Create space for reflection
>   - **Driving results (the system):** delivering outcomes reliably and effectively
>     - Trade-offs
>     - Prioritization
>     - Move with urgency towards quick answers
>   - **Empowering without micromanaging:** moving decisions closer to the work
>     - Provide clarity on outcomes and constraints (AKA expectations)
>     - Give people room to choose how - distributes power
>   - **Making strong decisions:** choosing direction for the team
>     - Creates clarity from the top, so teams can execute - concentrating power
> - By leveraging different "contrasting" abilities, by nature, **leaders are inconsistent (purposefully)**
> - Different leadership styles for different situations
>   - Crisis needs decisive action
>   - New development needs experimentation and facilitation
>   - Routine process needs coordination and discipline

> [!summary] Cumulative Summary

>

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!note] Marginalia / Reflection
> According to "Google's Project Oxygen" someone who leads well can:
>
> - Be a good coach
> - Empower the team without micromanaging
> - Focus on productivity and results
> - Communicate effectively
> - Implment a clear vision and strategy
> - Make strong decisions

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!quote]
> Google's Project Oxygen dug exactly into this question. What they did was they studied their most effective managers, and they identified key qualities in each of those managers. They're able to coach others, they can empower but not micromanage, they can focus on productivity and drive results. They can also communicate well, articulate a clear vision or strategy, and they can make strong decisions when the situation calls for it. Now if you look at this list closely, you'll notice something interesting. Some of these seem to pull in opposite directions, be a good coach, but also focus on productivity and results, empower the team, but also make strong decisions. The thing is, these aren't contradictions. These are actually context‑dependent choices
