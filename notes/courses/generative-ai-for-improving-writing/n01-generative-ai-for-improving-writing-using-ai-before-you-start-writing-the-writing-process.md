<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!note] Marginalia / Reflection
> When AI is believed to be smarter than another how can one not simply accept all suggestions and feedback to be true and authoritative?
>
> AI sycophancy (sik-uh-fuhn-see), the tendency of LLMs to tailor their answers to one's bias and self-validation, does not provide true critique nor scrutiny one would need to draft strong arguments or well written pros.
>
> AI, by way of Large Language Models (LLMs), are as good as what it has been trained on, and by volume, will often output more text than what is required to be quickly understood.
>
> In addition, because LLMs are trained on such volume of text the LLMs will output the average; and, so your "unique voice" and "style" will be edited and revised to be average - void of your own unique life experiences, memories, lessons, friendships made, mistakes, etc. - all things people care about in key figures, characters, and loved ones.

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!note] Marginalia / Reflection
>
> - The instructor says that "AI can step in; not do the work for you" is in full denial of those who would do exactly that (copy and paste)
> - If you don't know where to begin - gather requirements: find out the who, what, when, where, why, and how
> - The problem isn't that you don't know where to begin - the problem is that you don't know the scope and requirements, the expectations, of the final product. With a solid understanding one can then divide and conquer problems into smaller, easy to manage, components
> - A start of any project, even writing endeavors, may induce analysis paralysis, a state of overthinking a decision to the point where one take no action at all, as it is the ignition cost that holds one back to even get moving
> - Why is it a priority to be "fast" to begin with, speed is relative, sometimes thinking things out, slowly, and methodically is exactly what one needs to do (being slow isn't a "bug")
