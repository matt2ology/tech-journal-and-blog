## Prompting for Brainstorming and Planning

### idkHAXhDWSem

> [!tldr] Cumulative Summary
> Ackmann argues that a strong prompt has 5 components (i.e. role, task, context, structure, and tone/audience) to orient the LLM to provide text is closer aligned to the situation and goal.

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!quote]
> A strong prompt usually includes 5 simple parts, a role, a task, context, structure, and the tone or the audience you're talking to. Think of it like this. You're telling the AI who it should be, what to do, why it matters, how to organize and format, and specifying an output, like who it's for.

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!note] Marginalia / Reflection
>
> The Anatomy of a Solid Prompt:
>
> - **Role:** Who the AI should act as (defines expertise/voice)
> - **Task:** What you want it to do (the main output)
> - **Context:** Background info: "who, what, why, when"
> - **Structure:** How you want the output formatted or organized
> - **Tone/audience:** Who the audience is and what tone/style you want
>
> How does one know if they provided the right context or the right amount of context for the AI to merit you a weaker or stronger feedback?
>
> In the time spent you already know the whole stack of who are involved, what the output is, all of the context, the expected structure, and the tone set for the respective audience.
>
> What is AI really helping you? By default you're prolong the writing process by trying to copy-and-paste your brain by typing away endlessly in hopes that you can always communicate clearly the first time.

### id0w2E4geojK

> [!tldr] Cumulative Summary
> LLMs requires strong, specific, contextual prompts, so that it can output better initial outputs; however, be cautious as your information can be exposed and be used as training material for the LLM company/vender.
>
> Use unidentifiable stand-ins to provide a similar contextual scenario and goal you desire the AI to help in brainstorming and drafting.

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!quote]
> Just remember, before sharing any real‑world context in a prompt, avoid including confidential or proprietary details. Never paste internal documents, client data, private financials, or personally identifiable information into AI tools, unless your organization's policy explicitly allows it. Instead, you can use anonymized examples when you practice, such as Globomantics for your company, or a regional nonprofit for a client. This keeps your practice realistic while maintaining professional privacy and compliance standards.

<!-- blockquote separator -->

> [!note] Marginalia / Reflection
>
> - By default you're devoiding the AI model the necessary key context that helps the text generator spit out the proper formatting, style, and words that are key to your very specific contextual example
> - You are expecting outputs to match real-world reality, yet you feed it fiction for your fanfiction of a report
> - **Contradictory:** be specific, but use generic, anonymized, unidentifiable scenarios to keep privacy and yield better starting outputs
>   - Even if you use "anonymized" scenarios you've created an account with the company and can fingerprint you and can get a clue of who, what, where you are; and, can study your online-fingerprint via cookies and other ways the non-tech savvy are oblivious to
>   - Anyone at some point in time or another will leek enough identifiable information anyways
